## 1. Headline Answers

- **No detectable effect of online sports-gambling legalization on eviction filings.** The current pretrends baseline (26 states, 17 treated / 9 never-treated) puts the overall CS-DiD ATT at **+0.020 log points (SE 0.057)** — not significant, and parallel trends is not rejected (joint Holm p = 0.170). [`output/pretrends_lottery_A_baseline/pretrends_summary.csv`]
- **The best state-level bound**, from the size-gate-first PAP decision rule, is an **MDE of 8.75% (vwatt) to 11.5% (ewatt), both wild bootstrap**, depending on which estimand the PAP commits to — see §2. Neither figure supports ruling out effects in the 5–10% range that the credit literature reports.
- **At county level, the calibrated workhorse is did2s (Gardner)** — well-calibrated (4% Type I, SE/SD 0.97) — while **CS-DiD is eliminated on power**: it has no finite MDE on the pre-registered grid (power curve is flat, never crosses the 80% target even at δ=0.20). [`output_blinded_power/county_cs_adaptive/size_gate.csv`, `mde_adaptive.csv`]
- **The one point estimate suggesting a possible effect**: county-level did2s restricted to pre-COVID-treated cohorts, NH excluded (a documented data artifact), gives **+9.1%** (coefficient +0.087, SE 0.045; 95% CI −0.001 to +0.175 **log points**, i.e. ≈ −0.1% to +19.1% in filings — the source memo's "+17.5%" upper bound is the raw log endpoint read as a percent) — borderline significant, and directionally consistent with Hollenbeck et al. (2025)'s credit-outcome findings. This is a robustness/exploratory result, not the pre-registered headline. [`output/border_diagnostic/HANDOFF.md`, Phase 7 table]
- Both readings are true at once: **the pooled/PAP-style test finds nothing, and a cohort-disaggregated exploratory cut finds a borderline-positive signal in 8 of 13 individual state cohorts** (+6% to +12% range). Neither dominates; report both.

## 2. PAP Decision Table (Shift12, NV-Free, Spliced Window)

Decision rule: **size gate first** (any estimator whose null rejection rate is materially off 5% is disqualified), then MDE among survivors picks the spec.

**Two levels, both main specs (standing decision, 2026-09-14).** The state-month panel and the county-month panel are run as **two co-equal headline specifications**, kept separate and never pooled. Neither is "the robustness arm". They answer the same question on different samples and neither dominates: the state arm has **35 clusters** on the 42-state panel and admits every non-LSC source (MD NH WI MI OR + the 7 Eviction Lab states); the county arm has **26 clusters and 1,180 units**, gains the counties a state's fixed basket drops (Miami-Dade, Harris, Rutherford, Ada, Kanawha), and loses every non-LSC state. Clusters bind, not observations. Each level carries its own size gate and its own MDE, and a spec is reported as headline only for the level it was gated on — a county cell must never be cited as a state MDE, or the reverse. ## 5. Standing Design Facts & Traps

- **The COVID splice breaks CS-DiD's contiguity assumption.** `att_gt` receives a consecutive rank index that treats the month before and after the COVID gap as adjacent — this is independent of estimator, and generalizes: CS calibration is far worse on spliced windows than on the clean post-COVID-only window (5.0% analytic size vs 18–25% on spliced/annual-aggregated windows).
- **NV must be excluded from every state-level control pool.** NV has legal sports betting since 1949 (pre-PASPA); it has no row in `sports_gambling_legalization_dates.csv` (absent, not NA) and is an always-treated state masquerading as a never-treated control. Use `utils/eviction_treatment_schedule.R`'s helpers — `EVICTION_STATE_UNIVERSE`, `PRE_PASPA_SPORTS_BETTING`, `eviction_adoption_outcomes()`, `eviction_control_states()`, `assert_no_pre_paspa_controls()` — do not re-derive the universe or the NV rule ad hoc. County-level work is immune (NV has no rows in `monthly_county_data_download.csv`).
- **Panel-era pairing rule.** The state panel was rebuilt 2026-08-20 ~01:26 to fix a 12-state double count, and extended 2026-08-28 with per-state basket starts (adds CO/HI/MS/NY/SC → 39 states; NY and CO are treated; every prior state-month is unchanged bit-for-bit). Never pair a pre-rebuild run (38 states) with a post-rebuild run, and treat 2026-08-28 as a further boundary for any state-level sample. **The 2026-09-14 boundary takes the panel to 42 states** (single-series rebuild + OR + WA + MI + trailing-partial censor; RUN_LEDGER boundary 8) — anything computed on a 38/39/40-state panel is pre-boundary — a fresh `run_name` is required after any panel/schedule edit, since scenario ids hash settings, not data.
- **CO is missing Denver — in our source and in the state's own filings dashboard (verified 2026-09-14).** `monthly_county_data_download.csv` carries 62 of Colorado's 64 counties; the two absent are **Denver (08031)** and Hinsdale (08053). Denver is the state's largest renter county, so the CO series is a state total with its biggest renter county removed — `docs/STATE_SOURCES.md` shows "15 of 64" but not which county is gone. The cause is structural, not an LSC gap: Denver's evictions are heard in Denver County Court, administered separately from the state courts, so Colorado's own public filings dashboard also omits Denver (its district axis runs 1, 3, 4 … 22 — District 2 is absent). Denver appears only on that dashboard's closure-date pages, and those are closure-keyed from 2024-07-01. Details: [docs/findings-archive/2026-09-14_colorado-monthly-fed-dashboard.md](docs/findings-archive/2026-09-14_colorado-monthly-fed-dashboard.md).
- **The county LP-DiD arm is tighter than the state arm but its analytic inference is invalid (run 2026-09-14, `lpdid_shift12_clean_county_ewatt_pmd6`).** On the current clean design (shift12 / event_clean / pmd 6 / ewatt / ex NV,ID,HI,MS / spliced, 1,000 reps): county SD(att) **0.0297** vs state **0.0390** (ratio **0.763 [0.718, 0.818]**), null bias **+0.0164** vs **+0.0337** — and it wins on both *despite* having **26 clusters to the state arm's 33** (the county download is LSC-only, so WI, LA, MA, MD, MO and RI drop out). But mean SE 0.0254 gives **SE/SD 0.85**, and **analytic size at .05 is 0.157 — a hard size-gate FAIL** (state analytic: 0.045 PASS, SE/SD 1.14). Under the PAP decision rule the county analytic arm is disqualified, so **the SD advantage cannot be cited as an MDE**; cashing it requires a size-valid wild arm. Two things gate any use: the two runs are **not rep-paired** (the scenario hash includes `level`, so seed offsets differ), and the county `ewatt` estimand equally weights ~1,180 counties rather than 33 states, so it is **a different target parameter**. **VINTAGE CAVEAT (found after the run):** the state comparator `lpdid_shift12_clean_gate_ewatt_pmd6` is dated 2026-08-28, i.e. **pre**-single-series-rebuild, while the county arm reads `monthly_county_data_download.csv`, which that rebuild never touched. So this comparison **crosses the 2026-09-14 boundary** and violates the pairing rule. The rebuild added RI +77 months, MO/NM +41, MA +31 and added Oregon outright, taking the panel to 40 states and switchers 13 → 16, all of which should *lower* the post-rebuild state SD — so **0.763 is an upper bound on the county advantage, not an estimate of it.** The within-vintage comparator is the QUEUED `lpdid_clean_gate_ewatt_pmd6_v20260914`; run it before citing any ratio. The result is also design-dependent — it reverses `lpdid_launch_h1824_sdprobe_county_pmd12` (county SD 0.1065 vs state 0.080) on the launch/h18–24/pmd12 mature window.
- **The simulator's eligibility window was mis-specified in both directions; (18, 0) is the setting that matches the estimator (2026-09-14).** `.eligible_states()` is an **endpoint** test — a state is eligible for cohort date d iff its series starts at or before d−min_pre and ends at or after d+min_post — so it never checks that the months the estimator needs are present, and interior COVID holes are invisible to it. Under the headline `event_clean` anchor d = launch+12, the strict PMD=6 baseline's first month is launch−6 = d−18, and outcomes run launch+12..launch+24 = d..d+12 with a state contributing **at whatever horizons it has**. So the correct window is **min_pre 18, min_post 0** — not (12,6), which admitted states whose series begin as late as launch (no baseline at all) while demanding six post-months nothing uses, and not (18,12), which is infeasible. On the current 40-state panel all three admit the same **23** treated states, but the states that actually **contribute** go from **~11.0 (48%) at (12,6) to ~15.3 (66%) at (18,0)** — a 39% gain in contributing clusters for free. **The 0/25 failures are structural, not a data quirk**: the template slides over [panel_min+min_pre, panel_max−min_post], so widening either bound shortens that window from both ends and cuts candidate anchors (17 → 11 → 5 → 1) until none admits a full 23-slot matching; the seed only shuffles anchor order, which is why the result is 25/25 or 0/25 with nothing in between. `n_treated_clusters` still overstates the contributing count — the residual span-vs-presence gap needs a presence check, not a window setting.
- **The composition QA gate measures basket STABILITY, not REPRESENTATIVENESS (audited 2026-09-14).** FL, TX, TN, GA, ID, WV and CO are all marked `composition_risk = ok`, `min_basket_share = 1`, zero months below 90% — while FL's basket holds **45.3%** of its feed's renters (no Miami-Dade, no Broward, no Orange), TX's holds **48.3%** (no Harris, no Dallas, no Bexar) and ID's holds **5.4%** (no Ada). The mechanism is retroactive: the basket window runs to 2024-12 and requires a county to report in *every* month its state reports, so a county that stops reporting in 2024 (Harris 2024-05, Miami-Dade and Lee 2023-12, Rutherford 2024-02) is removed from that state's series **back to 2016**, not lost at the dropout date. **FL is the one that bites** — it is a Dec-2023 switcher whose two largest counties leave the feed the same month treatment begins. Full table and the absent-from-feed vs excluded-by-basket distinction: [docs/findings-archive/2026-09-14_missing-major-counties-audit.md](docs/findings-archive/2026-09-14_missing-major-counties-audit.md); audit script `analysis/diagnostics/missing_major_county_audit.R`.
- **Basket-composition QA gate.** Every state-panel row now carries `n_counties` and `basket_share`; `data/processed/state_panel_coverage_qa.csv` grades each state's reporting-county composition stability. Censor the tail or fix the basket before citing any state-level long-difference or level estimate — composition churn (e.g., FL losing Miami-Dade/Lee at 2024-01, TX losing Harris at 2024-05) produces artifacts of up to ±1 log point.
- **`lpdid_weights` / `lpdid_pmd` knobs** (plumbed config → grid → spec → estimator): `lpdid_weights` is `ewatt` (default, equally-weighted ATT = CS's estimand) | `cellsize` (retired, dead estimand — pre-2026-08-24 default) | `vwatt` (variance-weighted, Cengiz-style stack). `lpdid_pmd` sets base-period width (default 3).
- **The `event_clean` windows are anchored on TWO DIFFERENT dates, and getting it wrong is silent (2026-09-14).** Baseline is `[launch − pmd, launch − 1]`, indexed at `pseudo_launch`; outcome is `[g + hmin, g + hmax]` with `g = shift12 = launch + 12`, i.e. `[launch+12, launch+24]`. See `.re_lpdid()`'s `event_clean` branch (`R/power/run_estimators.R`): the outcome is `Y[, tt + h]` with `tt = gm` while the baseline is `B[, bb]` with `bb = lm`. **Anchoring both windows on shift12 places each of them 12 months too late** and returns a plausible-looking but wrong switcher set — it reports MI as outcome-blocked (2/13) when MI is baseline-blocked with a complete 13/13 outcome window, and it reports RI/IN/OR/NH as COVID casualties when they are kept. Do not hand-derive these offsets: `analysis/diagnostics/clean_design_window_audit.R` reads them from the config and asserts them.
- **The drift metric's norm coverage silently hid the cost of moving the COVID boundary (2026-09-14).** The seasonal norm required two years of 2017–19 coverage, which drops LA, NH and NY (series start 2019-01) — exactly the states an earlier boundary admits. So moving the drop end 2021-07 → 2021-06 appeared to add 3 switchers at unchanged SD(drift). Scored with a one-year norm (which leaves every already-scored state bit-identical), those three carry drifts of **+0.852 (NY), +0.457 (LA), −0.476 (NH)** and SD(drift) goes **0.161 → 0.331**, worsening SD/√n by 80%. NY and LA would enter with baselines in [2021-07, 2021-12] — New York's own moratorium ran to January 2022. **Standing law: large drift appears whenever one window is ramp-contaminated and the other is not** — baseline-side for NY/LA/TN/VA, outcome-side for OR (−0.940), IN (−0.390), NH. The LIVE 2021-07 boundary is the point at which no state has a ramp month in either window under the strict rule.
- **`cs_safe_patches` / `cs_faster_mode` flags** (`R/power/` CS runs). `cs_safe_patches: TRUE` is required to avoid uncatchable C-level process aborts from zero-row design matrices; `cs_faster_mode: FALSE` is required for CS to actually estimate on this design (the historical `TRUE` default failed on 100% of probed cells). Any pre-2026-08-17 CS run without both flags set correctly used a non-estimating code path and should not be cited.
- **LP-DiD analytic df now counts contributing clusters (fixed 2026-09-16).** `.re_lpdid()` used the panel-wide `cl$n_total − 1` for its t-inference; states placed but contributing no stack row (COVID hole in the baseline, series ending before launch+12) inflated the df and narrowed the analytic CI (~1–2% on the t-critical). It now uses the states that reach `feols()`. Every LP-DiD **analytic** size/MDE computed before 2026-09-16 — including the §2 "analytic passes in both weightings, 0.045/0.048" decision point — is slightly anti-conservative; wild rows are unaffected. Fresh `run_name` for any post-fix analytic run. Note: [docs/findings-archive/2026-09-16_lpdid-analytic-df-correction.md](docs/findings-archive/2026-09-16_lpdid-analytic-df-correction.md).

## 6. Open Questions

- **Annual data and LP-DiD: partially salvageable, but as a different estimand, not a rescue (probed 2026-09-14).** **Established:** the current spec run on an annualized panel fails with `"no identified (t, h) cells (all switchers lost to gap/edges)"` — `.re_lpdid` computes lags on **true calendar months from `month_date`, never the consecutive index** (`run_estimators.R:18`), which is exactly the feature that makes it COVID-gap-safe where CS-DiD is not, and exactly what breaks on 12-month spacing: the strict PMD=6 baseline needs 6 consecutive calendar months and annual data has 11 of 12 NA. **The right lever is the gap-tolerant regime** (`lpdid_pmd_min`, which averages the last pmd *observed* periods rather than calendar months) plus `lpdid_base_gapmax`; - **How the comparison literature handles COVID — read 2026-09-14 from `Literature/`. NONE of the three drops COVID months, and all three use outcomes that were not administratively suppressed. That is a positioning argument, not just a caveat.**
  - **Hollenbeck, Larsen & Proserpio, "The Financial Consequences of Legalized Sports Gambling"** — sample **2016-03 to 2023-06**, ~90M quarterly observations, credit-bureau outcomes (credit scores, bankruptcy filings, debt in collections). **No COVID exclusion.** Instead they run a BALANCE TEST: they take the Oxford COVID-19 Government Response Tracker indices (economic support, government response, stringency) as outcomes in their own DiD and find insignificant differences, "alleviating concerns that differences in COVID-19 response policies could bias our estimates." **That is precisely the design of this session's moratorium-enforcement test — same logic, same null, same conclusion.**
  - **Baker, Balthrop, Johnson, Kotter & Pisciotta, "Gambling Away Stability"** — sample **2018-08 to 2023-09**, 26 online legalizations, transaction-level household data from a financial aggregator. **No COVID exclusion and no COVID balance test.** COVID enters only as a covariate (stimulus payments, child tax credit).
  - **Goss & Mangrum, "Sports Betting Across Borders"** — NY Fed Consumer Credit Panel + Earnest Analytics. The most deliberate handling: ETWFE with **ω weights set to zero** for (i) treatment cohorts legalizing **before 2020:Q1** and (ii) the **2020:Q2** event-study parameters (American sports were cancelled). Their rationale is that these represent a **different treatment environment** — betting platforms had not ramped up pre-pandemic — **not that the outcome is contaminated.** Unrestricted results in their Online Appendix A.4.
  - **The critique paper ("How Bad Sports Betting Research Can Lead to Bad Sports Betting Regulations") attacks exactly this gap.** On Baker et al.: because gamblers are more likely to have children and COVID hit families with children harder, "it is feasible that the ill-effects Baker et al. attribute to gambling legalization are merely artefacts of the adverse financial impacts from COVID-19, which struck during the chronological middle of their sample period." On Hollenbeck: "given the immense impacts of COVID-19 ... it is difficult to imagine there were no shocks to economic activity that were non-randomly distributed to U.S. states and counties."
  - **Implication for this project.** Their outcomes — credit scores, deposits, delinquency, bankruptcy — kept accruing through the pandemic. **Eviction filings were legally blocked.** So the COVID baseline problem is structurally more severe here than in the literature we build on, which is why they could proceed and we cannot. Goss & Mangrum's choice is also the mirror image of ours: they drop PRE-2020 cohorts, whereas our pre-2020 cohorts (WV PA RI IN NH OR) are the ones with clean baselines and COVID-hit outcomes. **Worth stating explicitly in the PAP: dropping the COVID window is a departure from the comparison literature, and the justification is the administrative suppression of the outcome, not differential policy exposure (which we tested and, like Hollenbeck, found null).**
