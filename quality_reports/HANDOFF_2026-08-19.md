### 0.1 Two different estimators share the name "long differences"

**(A) What is IMPLEMENTED in the framework (`longdiff` arm, commit `949219e`):**
the *cohort-specific* design audited 2026-08-03
(`quality_reports/cohort_specific_long_difference_code_review.md`, 82/100):

- per treated state, baseline mean over `g-30..g-7`, post mean over `g..g+11`
  (headline), `g+12..g+23`, `g+24..g+35` (dynamic rows at event_time 18/30);
- controls transformed onto **each cohort's own calendar windows**;
- equal-treated-state aggregation; delete-one-state jackknife inference
  (`inference = "jack"`); coverage rule `ld_min_frac = 0.5`.
- Implementation: `R/power/run_estimators.R::.re_longdiff`. TDD'd
  (`tests/testthat/test-estimators.R`), 0.072 s/cell after integer-month-index
  optimisation (37x speedup, values bit-identical — verified).

**(B) What Ben ACTUALLY wants simulated (stated 2026-08-19, verbatim intent):**

```
tau_hat = (Ybar^T_end - Ybar^C_end) - (Ybar^T_pre - Ybar^C_pre)
```

- treatment is a **binary group**: "online legalized by 2022" — NOT staggered;
- **one common calendar** baseline window and endpoint window for every state;
- a single 2x2; exposure duration differs across states by construction (a
  2018 legalizer has ~7 years by the endpoint, a 2021 one ~4) — that is the
  intended long-run cumulative estimand, and it sidesteps staggered-DiD
  problems entirely;
- county-level, treatment applied to every county in a pseudo-treated state,
  population/renter weights and covariates if pre-specified;
- inference: state CRVE, randomization inference, wild cluster bootstrap;
- outputs: `P(p<.05 | tau=0)` (size), power curve, MDE-80, **plus sign errors
  (Type S) and magnitude exaggeration (Type M) conditional on significance**
  (Black et al. 2022);
- **plus Ben's addition**: compare averaging windows — 1 vs 3 vs 12-month
  endpoint averages; 2025-only vs 2025-26; 2017-18 vs 2018-only baseline —
  motivated by Burlig-Preonas-Woerman: serial correlation means more months do
  not buy information proportional to T. The sims should measure empirically
  how much averaging actually reduces the long difference's variance.

**(A) is NOT (B).** (A) is event-time aligned and staggered; (B) is
calendar-aligned and binary. (B) is NOT YET BUILT. Ben confirmed he wants (B),
with **endpoint = the 12 months ending 2025-09** (his choice, made because
there is no 2026 data yet).

Type S / Type M / believability already come free: `summarize_oc()` computes
`sign_error_rate`, `exaggeration_ratio`, `believable_share`.

### 0.2 The blinding constraint on (B) — explained, because it confused things

Ben asked, reasonably: "we have data to end-2024 and later, why would ending
there crush my power?" The answer, which must be stated this way:

**It does not crush his real power. It constrains the SIMULATION only.**

- The blinded framework may only touch **untreated** observations; each
  treated state is truncated ~7 months before its own launch. That is what
  makes a null simulation genuinely null.
- ~23 of 38 panel states are treated, nearly all before 2024. So in the
  **blinded** panel they contribute **no 2024-25 months at all** — a protocol
  rule, not a data gap.
- The RAW panel is healthy: **31 states have 2025 data**; 26-31 states are
  observed in every month through **2025-09**; coverage then collapses to 10
  states (2025-10) and 9 (2025-11) from reporting lag. **There is no 2026
  data** — which is why the endpoint was pinned to the 12 months ending
  2025-09.
- Blind-usable states (>=80% coverage in BOTH windows), measured:

  | baseline -> endpoint | usable states |
  |---|---|
  | **2017-18 -> 2024-10..2025-09 (Ben's calendar)** | **6** |
  | 2016-17 -> 2022-10..2023-09 | 7 |
  | 2016-17 -> 2019 | 19 |
  | 2016-17 -> 2018-07..2019-06 | 20 |
  | 2016 -> 2018 | **23** |

- The REAL analysis is unaffected: ~22 treated states have 2025 data and are
  exactly what the estimator wants. Only the *rehearsal* ("pretend treatment
  hit random states") cannot be staged at that calendar, because 6 states can
  only tell you about a 6-state design.

### 0.3 The agreed plan for (B) — NOT yet implemented

1. **Variance-structure exercise** (the Burlig question) on clean early
   windows where 19-23 states are available (e.g. baseline 2016-01..2017-12,
   endpoint 2018-01..2018-12). Vary endpoint length (1 / 3 / 12 months) and
   baseline length (12 / 24 months); estimate sigma = SD of the state-level
   long difference for each configuration. This is where the real information
   about averaging vs serial correlation lives.
2. **Era check**: re-estimate sigma on the 6-9 never-treated states that DO
   have 2024-25 data, to test whether borrowing sigma across eras is
   defensible (post-COVID drift is larger — see §3).
3. **MDE at REAL cluster counts**: combine sigma with the actual ~22 treated /
   ~12 control split rather than the simulated subsample's counts. Report the
   literal 6-state simulation as a **floor**, never as the headline.
4. Full Black et al. outputs at every step: size, power curve, MDE-80, Type S,
   Type M.

**Open decision Ben has NOT answered:** is randomization inference primary, or
state CRVE with wild bootstrap as backup? (RI is attractive here — binary
group assignment makes it exact and cheap.)

**Suggested build:** a standalone script
(`analysis/diagnostics/longdiff_2x2_power.R`) reusing `load_blinded_panel()`
and `impose_effect()`, rather than a new framework arm — the design is a
cross-sectional 2x2 on per-county long differences (`dY_c = post mean - pre
mean`), so it does not fit `make_pseudo_schedule`'s date-based designs. A
binary pseudo-assignment can be faked by giving all pseudo-treated states a
COMMON `pseudo_g` = endpoint start, then `impose_effect(shape="step")` lands
the effect exactly on the endpoint window.
