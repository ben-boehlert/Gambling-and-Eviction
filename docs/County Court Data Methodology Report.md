* In 2017, Callie Weir (Research Analyst I) wrote the following in response to a request for Unlawful Detainer/Forcible Entry and Landlord/Tenant Disputes data:


“The ORDA at the Judicial Council Administrative Office of the Courts collects case  
count data for juvenile court, magistrate court, municipal court, probate court, state court, and  
superior court. Unlawful Detainer/Forcible Entry is not a distinct case category in Georgia,  
however, given that it is related to Landlord/Tenant Disputes, any instances of Unlawful  
Detainer/Forcible Entry would necessarily fall under Dispossessory Actions in magistrate, state,  
and superior courts. Attached is the available data for magistrate, state, and superior courts.

In magistrate court, an eviction may be brought as a Dispossessory Action…Magistrate courts have jurisdiction over Dispossessory Actions up to monetary amounts of $15,000, but no jurisdiction over cases where affirmative equitable relief is sought between landlord and tenant.

The Administrative Office of the Courts began collecting Dispossessory Action data in  
superior court in 2009\. Prior to 2009, superior court data was collected for felonies,  
misdemeanors, probation violations, and appeals. Changes in reporting and data collection are  
based on the needs of the judiciary and continuous improvement in data collection and analysis.  
The data attached contains numbers for Dispossessory Actions filed in years 2009-2013 and  
numbers for Dispossessory Actions that were open, filed, and disposed from 2014-2016. Each  
year, with the exception of 2016 where all courts reported, some courts did not report or did not  
report numbers for Dispossessory Actions. While superior courts have jurisdiction over  
Dispossessory Actions, the numbers indicate that Dispossessory Actions are more frequently  
filed in state and magistrate courts.”

* However, in more recent years it seems that **with the exception of Fulton County** the vast majority of cases are filed through Magistrate Court

**Notes: “**One thing to flag: 2014 shows a notable dip to 178K statewide (vs. \~267K in 2013 and 2015). That's the transition year where magistrate court switched from the Open column to the Filed column — if magistrate's Filed count was genuinely lower than Open that year, the drop is real; if not, it might mean the 2014 format is slightly different than expected. The DB had higher values for several 2014 counties (flagged in the build output as discrepancies), which points toward the extract undercounting 2014\. Worth checking if you see downstream issues.”

There are also questions for Fulton 2003 and other early years. Are we getting data from Magistrate and Superior Court?

## **38\. Oregon** 

In Oregon, evictions may be filed in either circuit or justice courts, and the distribution of fillings between these court systems varies by county. Critically, ease of data access is determined by where cases are filed. Data on circuit court cases are centrally compiled and accessible through the OJCIN OnLine (Oregon Judicial Case Information Network) portal maintained by the Oregon Judicial Department.[^1] In contrast, data on eviction cases processed by justice courts must be directly obtained from individual courts, which makes it harder to determine overall eviction rates in counties where evictions may be filed in justice courts.[^2]

The data in our database were provided by the community organization *Don’t Evict Pdx*, whose volunteers collected the records by scraping the OJCIN Online portal.[^3] Since the portal only contains records of circuit court cases, the filing figures are undercounts in many of the counties, except in the 22 counties where eviction filings occur exclusively in circuit courts.[^4] Researchers at *Evicted in Oregon* published a list identifying such counties, which we used to flag the remaining counties as incomplete through the variable `incomplete_county`. 

Importantly, since we only report eviction counts from circuit, overall statewide eviction rates cannot be determined using this database.
